"A graphics card is like a huge kitchen with hundreds of chefs. The chefs can chop, mix, and plate incredibly fast, but they can only work as fast as ingredients arrive through the kitchen pass. The pass is the memory bandwidth: the rate at which data can move between the GPU’s fast little pantry (cache) and the big warehouse (main memory). If the pass is narrow, chefs stand around waiting, no matter how skilled they are.

This happens because many graphics tasks need lots of data. Rendering a frame means reading textures, vertex positions, colors, and writing pixels. The compute units may be able to do trillions of math operations per second, but each operation often needs a byte or more of data from memory. If the memory system can only deliver, say, 500 gigabytes per second, then the GPU cannot usefully do more than about 500 billion operations per second if each operation needs one byte.
